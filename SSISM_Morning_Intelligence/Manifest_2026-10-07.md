## Abstract

The Systemic Structural Intelligence Safety Management (SSISM) system introduces a design paradigm in which **capability must remain strictly subordinate to verification**. As artificial intelligence capabilities scale exponentially, conventional systems risk converting uncertainty into false certainty. SSISM enforces a structured barrier whereby action, inference, and execution are constrained by objective verification metrics and institutionalized human oversight.

This paper formalizes the core epistemic axioms, the Digital Trust Score \( \Phi \) derived from a sigmoid logistic transformation of aggregated risk, the Mandatory Lockout Protocol, and the five-stage Stage-4 Epistemic Taxonomy. The governing principle is expressed as the ordered cascade:

> **Increasing AI Capability → Increasing Verification → Institutionalized Human Responsibility**

### Digital Trust Score Equation

The Digital Trust Score \( \Phi \) is obtained by a sigmoid (logistic) transformation of the Total Risk Aggregation Factor \( Z \):

\[ \Phi = \frac{1}{1 + e^{Z}} \]

*(Equation 1 — Digital Trust Score)*

The sigmoid maps any real-valued risk aggregate into the open interval \( (0, 1) \). High positive \( Z \) drives \( \Phi \) toward $0$ (low trust → lockout); large negative \( Z \) drives \( \Phi \) toward $1$ (high trust → proceed).

### Total Risk Aggregation Factor

The Total Risk Score \( Z \) aggregates five weighted threat-vector variables minus a systemic bias threshold:

\[ Z = w_A \cdot A + w_U \cdot U + w_L \cdot L + w_R \cdot R + w_T \cdot \Delta T - \theta \]

*(Equation 2 — Total Risk Aggregation)*

## III. SSISM Stage-4 Epistemic Taxonomy

To prevent subjective fear or speculative bias from driving intelligence assessments, information must be parsed through the SSISM Stage-4 Verification Framework before any claim is elevated to actionable status.

| Stage | Taxonomy Category | Operational Definition | Example Application |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **1** | OBSERVED / SOURCED | Empirically demonstrated, cryptographically verified, or independently documented fact | AI-generated/modified code committed to repository with valid SHA-256 hash |
| **2** | CALCULATED / SOURCED | Derived mathematically from verified primary sources with clear confidence intervals | Statistical performance benchmarks on documented domain datasets |
| **3** | INFERRED / POSSIBLE | Logically consistent hypothesis supported by observable trends but lacking direct proof | Model capacity expansion leading to specific domain performance increases |
| **4** | NOT YET ESTABLISHED | Theoretical construct requiring future testing; currently lacks empirical backing | Autonomous recursive self-improvement loops in live production environments |
| **5** | FUTURE SCENARIO / HIGH-IMPACT HYPOTHESIS | Probabilistic foresight projection or extreme tail-risk event; not automatic evidence | Uncontrolled capability divergence or existential risk scenarios |

### Illustrative Classification of Common AI-Capability Claims

| Claim | Classification |
|-------|----------------|
| AI wrote/modified code | **OBSERVED / SOURCED** *(if demonstrated)* |
| AI can perform some AI-research tasks | **OBSERVED / SOURCED** *(if independently documented)* |
| AI might improve its own capabilities | **INFERRED / POSSIBLE** |
| AI will recursively improve itself | **NOT YET ESTABLISHED** |
| AI will escape human control | **FUTURE SCENARIO** |
| AI will cause human extinction | **HIGH-IMPACT HYPOTHESIS** *(not automatically evidence)* |

When confronted with the question *“Should we be scared of AI?”*, SSISM redirects inquiry to:

> *“What exactly happened? What evidence supports the claim? What remains unknown?”*

The ordered pipeline  
**OBSERVED → CALCULATED → INFERRED → NOT YET ESTABLISHED → FUTURE SCENARIO**  
supplies a natural epistemic structure that resists premature certainty.
